The shape of every one of these is the same four commands: pick the column with `awk '{print $N}'`, then `sort`, then `uniq -c`, then `sort -rn`. Work out which `$N` you need by counting fields on one line — `head -1 access.log | tr ' ' '\n' | cat -n` numbers them for you.
The address is field 1, the path is field 7, the status is field 9, and the response size is field 10. Field 4 is `[18/Oct/2026:14:22:07`, with the leading bracket still attached, which is why the hour needs splitting rather than cutting.
For the hour: `split($4, a, ":"); print a[2]` in awk gives you two digits. The timestamp has three colons in it, so splitting on colon puts the date in `a[1]` and the hour in `a[2]`.
Summing a column is awk's other trick: `awk '{s += $10} END {print s}'`. If the number comes out far too small, check whether the field you are summing is the one with the bytes in it rather than the status.
Question 5 is two steps and the order matters: filter to the 404s *first*, then count by address. Counting first and filtering second answers a different question, and the busiest address overall is not the one that generates the most 404s — which is the whole reason the question is worth asking.
When you have a pipeline you like, keep it. These six are the beginning of the file you will reach for every time something is wrong with a web server, and the second time you write them from scratch will not be faster than the first.
