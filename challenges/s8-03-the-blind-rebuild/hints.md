Taking a hint here is not free in the way it is elsewhere: the hints are documentation, and reading them is the thing the challenge asks you not to do. If you are stuck, the intended move is to write the gap down, work it out from the machine, and keep going. These lines are for afterwards.
Afterwards, then: the gaps that show up in a blind rebuild are almost never the big steps. They are the exact `rsync` flags, the name of the drop-in directory, which unit to reload rather than restart, and the order of two commands where one silently depends on the other. Runbooks written from memory have the big steps and none of those.
The machine will tell you most of what you need if you ask it rather than search: `systemctl cat <unit>` for what a unit actually says, `sshd -T` for what sshd will actually do, `nginx -T` for the whole merged configuration, `journalctl -u <unit> -n 50` for why something would not start. A runbook that says "check the logs" is not a runbook; one that names the command is.
`history` on the old machine is the best first draft of a runbook, and it is on the old machine. If you want it for next time, that is itself a gap to write down — and the fix is to write the runbook during the build, not after it.
When the check says the runbooks have no commits since the baseline, the usual cause is not that nothing was missing: it is that the notes are sitting uncommitted, or in a file outside `S8_RUNBOOK_DIR`, or in a terminal scrollback that is about to close. `git -C "$S8_RUNBOOK_DIR" status` first.
If you truly needed nothing, commit the date and the fact. A runbook carrying "exercised blind, 2026-03-14, no gaps found" is substantially more trustworthy than the same text without it, and that line is the finding.
